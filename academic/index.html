About

Memory in ordering processes

I am a physicist at the Instituto de Física, UFRGS, in Porto Alegre, working on nonequilibrium statistical mechanics. I am completing my PhD under the supervision of Jeferson J. Arenzon, co-supervised by Wellington G. Dantas of the Universidade Federal Fluminense, and my defense is scheduled for October 2026.

My doctoral work concerns the effect of memory on an ordering process. During my Master's, we proposed the persistent voter model, in which each agent carries a confidence that rises whenever it meets agreement and is erased by a single disagreement. Zealots produced by the dynamics fill the cores of the domains and give the interfaces an effective surface tension. Alongside opinion dynamics, we studied percolation in intercellular metabolic networks with Andrea De Martino and Daniele De Martino, a collaboration that included a three-month research visit to the Politecnico di Torino.

Research

Research lines

  • Opinion inertia and self-induced zealots

    A microscopic rule for confidence turns a model without surface tension into one that coarsens by curvature. The confidence increment tunes the model continuously between ordering under interfacial noise alone, as in the voter model, and curvature-driven coarsening, as in the Ising model at zero temperature. We study the crossover between the two regimes and the consensus-time distribution on either side of it.

    voter model · zealots · opinion dynamics · consensus times

  • Interface geometry, coarsening and finite-size scaling

    Pinned interfaces carry a geometry of their own, set by the joint statistics of the main interface and the inner ones. We measure their fractal dimensions and the dimensionless combinations that collapse the two-dimensional data. Alongside the simulations, we build minimal random-walk and master-equation models of confined walks with fluctuating boundaries, from which the widths and exponents follow analytically.

    coarsening · pinned interfaces · finite-size scaling · master equations

  • Percolation in cross-feeding metabolic networks

    Cells that overflow metabolites feed one another, and the resulting exchange network can be read as a percolation problem. With Andrea and Daniele De Martino, we study the transitions these networks undergo as overflow and environment vary, and what the percolating component means for the community that produces it.

    percolation · metabolic networks · complex systems

Methods

Kinetic and stationary Monte Carlo simulation, including Dobrushin boundary conditions, master equations, mean-field closures and coarse-graining, finite-size scaling, percolation analysis and fractal-dimension estimators. Simulations are written in C and C++ with OpenMP, and the analysis in Python.
