About

Order that forms, spreads, and gets stuck

I work on the statistical physics of nonequilibrium systems. Most of my research concerns the persistent voter model, a variant of the voter model in which agents accumulate confidence through agreement and, past a threshold, have their opinions momentarily frozen — becoming self-induced zealots. Those zealots nucleate in the cores of the domains, the agents who may still change their minds are confined to a narrow active region around the interfaces, and the model acquires an effective surface tension it would otherwise lack. A second line, developed with Andrea De Martino and Daniele De Martino, treats percolation transitions in the cross-feeding networks that cells form when they exchange metabolites.

I completed my doctorate in physics at the Instituto de Física, UFRGS, in 2026, supervised by Jeferson J. Arenzon and co-supervised by Wellington G. Dantas, and hold a master's degree (2022) and a bachelor's degree (2019) in physics from the same institute. My doctoral work was supported by a CNPq fellowship, and my master's by CAPES.

Research

Three lines

  • Opinion inertia and self-induced zealots

    How a microscopic rule for confidence — agreement reinforces, disagreement erodes — turns a model without surface tension into one that coarsens by curvature. The questions here are the crossover between voter-like, fluctuation-driven dynamics and Ising-like, curvature-driven dynamics, and the shape of the consensus-time distribution on either side of it.

    voter model · zealots · opinion dynamics · consensus times

  • Interface geometry, coarsening and finite-size scaling

    The geometry of pinned interfaces: the joint statistics of the main interface and the inner ones, fractal dimensions, and the dimensionless combinations that collapse the two-dimensional data. Alongside the simulations I build minimal random-walk and master-equation models — confined walks with fluctuating boundaries — to derive the widths and exponents analytically rather than fit them.

    coarsening · pinned interfaces · finite-size scaling · master equations

  • Percolation in cross-feeding metabolic networks

    Cells that overflow metabolites feed one another, and the resulting exchange network can be read as a percolation problem. With Andrea and Daniele De Martino I study the transitions such networks undergo as the overflow and the environment vary, and what the percolating component means for the community that produces it.

    percolation · metabolic networks · complex systems

Methods

Kinetic and stationary Monte Carlo simulation, including Dobrushin boundary conditions; master equations, mean-field closures and coarse-graining; finite-size scaling; fractal-dimension estimators. Simulations in C and C++ with OpenMP, analysis in Python.
